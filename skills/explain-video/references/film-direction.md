# Direct the visual explanation

Use this while storyboarding and reviewing a narrated film. It owns visual craft; narration setup and export-clock mechanics have their own conditional references.

## Establish the subject before its machinery

Introduce the actual linked project or question and enough background to make its role understandable. A Mem0 link, for example, calls for identifying Mem0 as software developers integrate into AI applications to store and retrieve information from past interactions, then explaining how that fits between the application and language model. Verify the linked version and distinguish the open-source library from a managed service where relevant. A general story about an assistant remembering preferences does not identify the project.

For a change, establish the existing behavior, the limitation or requirement, the core idea of the solution and the resulting behavior. Then choose implementation details that explain that result. For a broader architecture, connect responsibilities and information flow rather than presenting a list of components. Use small semantic examples where they help; arithmetic is not a default.

## Make the argument visible

Choose a representation whose relationships support the claim. A system map, two lifecycles, aligned before/after lanes or a simplified interface can each fit a different question. Include representative messages or records when empty arrows would hide what moves. Reuse visual conventions where helpful without requiring one permanent diagram.

Keep entities recognizable through operations. A transformed value should retain its identity, and a changed view should connect to the preceding one through narration or a shared example. Explain a relevant omission or state change rather than letting an object vanish and reappear. Color may represent identity, role or state; make its meaning clear instead of assigning a universal color rule.

Show the condition that causes a change and enough of the prior state to inspect the consequence. Keep unaffected structure stable when comparing versions. Distinguish a logical dependency from completed work, and a lost reply from a failed operation. In authoring notes, check the plausible wrong reading of important diagrams and how the picture prevents it.

At section boundaries, establish what was learned and why the next question follows. A heading alone does not supply that causal connection. Keep any caveat that changes the mechanism's meaning near its claim; keep incidental implementation qualifications in source notes.

## Pace for inspection

Let the viewer read and trace a relationship after the relevant material has settled. Twenty to thirty seconds of useful stillness is acceptable. Add motion when it exposes an operation or missing connection; decorative activity cannot repair unclear reasoning. Clear a dense outgoing view before an unrelated one appears, and inspect the middle of transitions for superimposed diagrams.

Stage narration, labels and motion so they do not compete. Center phrase subtitles on the film's horizontal midpoint with safe space beneath the diagram. Inspect actual line geometry and reading time. Keep a complete qualification with its claim: splitting “rather than assume relevance ranking establishes what is true” must not leave a standalone caption asserting that ranking establishes truth.

Keep source-location labels, hashes, provider metadata, audit footers and source end cards off the film. Useful code excerpts remain part of the explanation. Preserve the selected warm-paper/ink styling. Music and sound effects are optional when requested or useful; speech alone is complete. If used, keep separate stems, duck beneath speech, avoid clipping and inspect the actual mix.

## Iterate cheaply and inspect the delivered film

Reuse approved scene treatment and working production code. Check the edited storyboard and representative stills before generating full narration or exporting. For a new treatment, a short slice covering a real change and its settled result is a useful production test. Reuse cached speech on visual-only edits.

Inspect the opening, decisive states, intermediate transformations, section handoffs and ending. Ask whether the viewer can identify the subject, locate the evidence in the picture, follow the same entity and explain the consequential relationship. Check labels for overlap, overflow and legibility at the intended viewing size. A fixed video artboard does not require building a responsive webpage.

Fully decode the final video and compare representative decoded frames with the renderer at matched timestamps. Check audio presence, continuity and timing against the assembled narration. A voice existing as WAV files is not proof the MP4 contains it. Keep technical checks separate from source fidelity and judgments of educational usefulness. Review observed failures rather than repeating full checks after unrelated text edits.
